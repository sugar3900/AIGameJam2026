# ARC-FLOOR-WOOD-01 — old honey-brown plank floor

A modular living/hall/bedroom/office wood floor with narrow staggered boards, continuous dark subfloor, and warm aged wood. The design target is `concepts/wood-floor-design.png` and the parent apartment image. This is one material family, not four different floors.

## Texture comparison

Three images in `textures/` were generated independently. Candidate A is strongly orange and uniformly saturated; B is darker, more realistic and less visibly painterly; C has broader painterly grain, warmer honey/amber color and some muted gray variation, so C is the base-color source. None is demonstrated to tile as a raw image. The model instead folds its U coordinates to mirror C at the 2 m module boundaries, with multiple V slices and six restrained color multipliers. Repetition is visible at room scale and needs an in-room assessment; do not describe the original image as seamless.

## Source, scale and materials

- `model/ARC-FLOOR-WOOD-01.blend`: editable scene with one source tile plus three duplicates named `PREVIEW repeat ...`, an optional wear plane and preview copies, camera and light for the 4 × 4 m render. `model/ARC_Floor_Wood_2m.fbx` exports only the single source tile. Rebuild with `blender -b --python model/build_floor.py`.
- FBX reimport dimensions: **2.000 × 2.000 × 0.029 m**. Surface top is Z = 0, origin centered in X/Y. Boards run along local X, 0.125 m wide; position tile centers on a 2 m grid at identical height and rotation. Trim edge tiles to the room shape rather than scaling them. Do not rotate tiles independently or their grain and edge UV continuity will not match. Trim/wall intersections and exact room fit remain to be checked.
- Six floorboard material slots all sample `textures/wood-candidate-c.png` as sRGB base color with linear RGB multipliers (1.03,1.00,.96), (.72,.75,.76), (.90,.84,.77), (.79,.83,.85), (.98,.88,.77), (.84,.86,.82). A seventh dark material is the subfloor seen through the joints. Blender roughness is 0.88; recreate each multiplier and roughness in Unreal because FBX does not preserve the complete node graph. UVs are already folded: use **clamp**, not extra world-space repeat, and preserve the imported UVs. Recreate the dark subfloor material; make a simple continuous collision box spanning each tile and confirm the collision is flush with the visible floor.
- `previews/floor-repeat-top.png` and `floor-repeat-perspective.png` show four touching 2 m tiles. In Blender the joints are visually continuous, with a natural plank gap at the horizontal tile boundary. `model/validate_fbx.py` reimports and checks geometry and UVs; the two X-edge lists match in position, material index and UV for all 24 boundary vertices per side. The Y edges are genuine plank seams: the subfloor boundary vertices match, and the board gaps each stop 0.0015 m before the shared edge. It reports 2 meshes, 232 vertices, 124 triangles, no missing UVs, nonfinite values or zero-area faces. This is not an Unreal import test.

Check color, roughness, pattern repetition and seams in the assembled apartment under its final lighting. Also verify the UV edge match and collision after actual Unreal FBX import; neither has been tested there yet.

## Optional nonrepeating wear

`model/ARC_Floor_Wear_Optional.fbx` is a separate, unlit-position-independent UV plane with the selected alpha texture `textures/wear-candidate-b.png`. A and B were generated independently; B gives softer muted scuffs, while A has sharper high-contrast flecks. The base floor does not include wear: place a few planes individually at varied positions, scales and angles to avoid repeating wear on the 2 m module grid. The preview uses four such placements. Recreate the wear material in Unreal using texture RGB and alpha multiplied by **0.22**, translucent or an appropriate deferred decal material; disable shadow casting and collision, and sit the plane slightly above the surface (preview 0.001 m). The exported plane is 0.9 × 0.6 m at Z = 0; the source scene moves it for preview. In-game blend settings, depth sorting and z-fighting remain untested. Validation reimports this separate FBX and confirms UVs and a nonconstant alpha channel.
