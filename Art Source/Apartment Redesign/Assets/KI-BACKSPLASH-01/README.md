# KI-BACKSPLASH-01 — kitchen checker backsplash

A warm cream and muted sage checker backsplash for the A+ kitchen north-wall counter. The exported module is **1.00 m wide × 0.56 m high × 0.034 m deep**, with five rows of 0.10 m pitch tiles, visible cream grout, top and bottom ceramic caps, and separate caps for the ends of a run. Local +X follows the wall, -Y faces the room, +Z is up. The module origin is its lower left at the back wall plane. The modeled proportions follow the elevation/inventory rather than the precise tile count in a generated image.

## Visual decisions and files

- `concepts/checker-a.png` and `concepts/botanical-b.png` are two independently generated concepts. The muted sage/ivory checker A was chosen for the elevation's checker description and visual relationship with sage cabinetry; B's floral accents are retained as an alternative. The exact color balance and density must be judged with the kitchen's cream/terracotta checker floor in the assembled room.
- `textures/glaze-ivory.png` and `glaze-sage.png` are independently generated single-surface painterly glazes, sampled within each tile with varied UV offsets and quarter rotations. They are not proven seamless full-surface textures; the mesh avoids sampling their edges, but the same 1 m pattern repeats. Per-tile material assignments and UVs are baked into the FBX. The original images are not a PBR material set.
- `model/KI_Backsplash_Module_1m.fbx` contains the tile field, backing and horizontal caps. `model/KI_Backsplash_End_Left.fbx` and `KI_Backsplash_End_Right.fbx` have the **same module origin** and extend 0.03 m beyond the module on their respective sides. For a run of N whole modules, place module origins at x=0, 1, ... N-1 m; place left cap at the first module origin and right cap at the last module origin. Caps have width 0.03 m, so include their extensions in the available run.
- Partial lengths need a cut/modified module or custom remainder; **do not stretch the entire 1 m module nonuniformly**, because tile pitch and grout will distort. Segment the wall around windows, range or other actual openings and fixtures; confirm the run with the engine assembly rather than assuming the whole 6.5 m wall is covered.
- `previews/backsplash-repeat.png` shows three modules and end caps with placeholder plaster, countertop and cabinetry; `backsplash-detail.png` shows the tile and grout close up. Preview geometry, camera and lights are included in the `.blend`; use the named FBX exports for importing individual assets.

## Unreal setup and verification

FBX shader graphs do not transfer reliably. Recreate `Warm ivory ceramic` from `glaze-ivory.png` mixed 30% toward linear tint `(0.78, 0.70, 0.57)`, and `Soft sage ceramic` from `glaze-sage.png` mixed 30% toward `(0.49, 0.55, 0.43)`; use roughness approximately 0.64–0.68. Assign the cream grout and glazed cap slots separately, referencing `model/build_backsplash.py` for their values. Preserve the two tile material slots and the UVs. Check the actual material under game lighting; tint values are Blender inputs and should be adjusted only after visual comparison in Unreal. Choose appropriate simple wall collision; avoid a separate blocking surface that impedes sink interaction.

`model/validate_fbx.py` reimports all three FBXs and validates dimensions, finite coordinates and UVs, nonzero triangles and material assignment. The field exports as 4 meshes / 828 triangles. The tile pattern has ten columns per metre, so adjacent module origins at exactly 1 m preserve checker parity. The test render shows joins, but this does **not** establish in-engine seamlessness, collision or appearance. In particular, inspect seams, lightmap artifacts, end caps, range and window intersections, and repetition in the complete room before final acceptance.
