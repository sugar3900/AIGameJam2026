# BA-TILE-01 — bathroom tile and repair patch

A repeatable blush ceramic tile field with warm grout and a plum cap, plus a small separate fracture overlay. The bathroom reference and the private-room elevations specify 1.15 m ordinary wainscot and 1.55 m tub surround. The fracture belongs above the tub, unobstructed and independently repairable; it is not mold or lead paint.

## Contents and design decisions

- `concepts/bath-tile-before-after.png` shows generated damaged and repaired views. Its cream cap differs from the reference; the built asset intentionally uses the reference's darker plum cap.
- `textures/candidate-a.png` and `candidate-b.png` are independently generated. A unintentionally includes baked grout and tile borders; B offers a single field of painterly warm glaze. B is selected and sampled in varied offsets and quarter-turn orientations per physical tile, avoiding A's duplicate grid. Eight subtle pink glaze material slots are assigned per tile; they survive FBX reimport, but the shader mixes themselves must be recreated in Unreal. This is a stylized color texture, not a claim that the file is a perfectly tileable PBR scan.
- `model/build_tile.py` builds the asset with Blender background mode and renders `previews/tile-before.png` / `tile-after.png`; two side-by-side modules make the 1 m repeat visible. The Blender file also contains these contextual preview copies, floor, lights and camera; export the named FBXs rather than the whole preview scene.
- `BA_Tile_1m_Module.fbx` is a 1.00 m wide × 1.10 m high field and backing, tile pitch 0.10 m; local +X runs along the wall, -Y faces the room and +Z is up. Place another at X +1.00 m. Because tile placement is geometrically seeded and identical per module, repeated patches are expected; inspect visible repetition and corners in the complete bathroom.
- `BA_Tile_Cap.fbx` is separately placed at Z=1.10 m for a total 1.15 m. For the tub surround, put `BA_Tile_040_Extension.fbx` at Z=1.10 m, and the cap at Z=1.50 m, for a total 1.55 m. Both run 1 m along the wall. Cut or author end sections at openings; do not nonuniformly stretch tile spacing.
- `BA_Tile_Damage.fbx` has the same local origin as the 1 m module. It is shallow geometry laid over an intact tile field; hide it on repair to reveal the unbroken surface. It is a visual approximation, not a physically recessed hole. Damage occupies approximately x=0.405–0.590 m, z=0.422–0.846 m. Move it to an unobstructed area above the tub, ensuring it does not cross a module seam, fixture or window.

## Integration and validation

`validate_fbx.py` reimports the four exports and checks finite coordinates and UVs, nonzero triangle area and expected bounds. The full-width before/after previews show the two-module repeat and the detail previews show damage and UV variation. The repeated preview has aligned joints with a deliberate joint at the 1 m module/cap boundary; inspect all joins and corners after placement, including the 0.4 m vertical extension. FBX does not carry Blender shader graphs reliably: create eight instances for the FBX material slots `Pink glaze 00` through `07` from a shared shader: lerp candidate B (sRGB color) toward the corresponding linear tint at 0.40, using palette values from `build_tile.py`, with roughness around 0.66. Preserve all eight face assignments on import; the per-tile UV offsets and 90-degree rotations are baked in the mesh. Recreate the warm ivory grout and plum cap, and configure the damage material separately. These eight slots have a rendering cost; consider a single shader with baked vertex colors during optimization, then compare its appearance before replacing this setup. FBX imports may use centimeters in Unreal; verify world scale and facing.

Unreal material, seam appearance under actual lighting, collision, fixture clearance and repair interactions have not been tested. Author the damage as a nonblocking attached actor tagged `RepairDamage` on the existing bath/tile malady, and confirm repair, save/load and reset behavior; do not assume the presence of this FBX creates a game interaction. Check backface visibility if using one-sided damage geometry and avoid placing a collision blocker over the interaction. The clean field is underneath, not a separate state mesh.
