# ENV-SKY-01 — painterly late-afternoon environment

**Status: provisional art handoff.** A warm brush-painted 360° sky with distant brick rowhouse roofs and tree canopy, designed for the A+ apartment's golden-hour lighting. The sky image itself was generated with the built-in imagegen tool using the approved painterly fixer-upper concept and `Art Source/Environment Textures/T_WindowView.png` as style/neighborhood references. The assets are not yet imported or validated in Unreal.

## Deliverables

- `textures/ENV-SKY-01_painterly_2k.png`: 2048 × 1024, 2:1 equirectangular PNG, intended for a sky-sphere/background material.
- `textures/ENV-SKY-01_painterly_2k_LDR-derived.hdr`: 2048 × 1024 Radiance RGBE, same painted panorama in linear-color form, intended as a candidate for Unreal's HDR/long-lat texture-cubemap import and SkyLight specified cubemap. **This file is LDR-derived, not a captured HDRI**: its generated input was 8-bit PNG and its highest saved channel is approximately 0.996. RGBE encoding does not recover clipped sun radiance or real exposure latitude. Keep the existing directional light for direct sunlight; tune SkyLight intensity, sky material and exposure in the actual scene.
- `concepts/sky-candidate-a.png`, `-b.png`, `-c.png`: three independent original generations. B was selected for its soft warm sky and low skyline. `sky-candidate-b-refined.png` is a later imagegen edit retained for comparison; the final output is baked from original B.
- `previews/sun_side.png`, `sun_disc.png`, `seam_side.png`, `side_a.png`, `side_b.png`, `zenith.png`: Blender perspective renders from inside the HDR environment. They do not show the apartment or establish Unreal appearance. `sun_side` is a horizon-facing view; `sun_disc` looks upward toward the painted sun; `seam_side` looks across the wrap.
- `tools/build_sky.py`, `validate_sky.py`, `render_preview.py`, `inspect_candidates.py`, `check_color.py`: reproducible headless Blender technical bake, checks and views. Run from the repository root with `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender -b -t 2 --python '<script path>'`.

The original generated image was **1774 × 887**. The 2K delivery is a bilinear resampling, not additional generated detail. The bake applies a modest latitude remap, a non-reflected 6% overlap blend at the horizontal seam, and a fade to a uniform color within the outermost 2.5% at each pole. This improves spherical behavior but slightly softens the seam; inspect the distant buildings in `seam_side.png` and in the actual windows for ghosting. The authored painting itself has repeating architectural motifs; the technical blend does not mirror the whole skyline.

## Orientation and lighting

- Equirectangular **top = zenith (+Z), bottom = nadir (−Z)**; geometric horizon is halfway down the image, `v = 0.5`. For file/image UVs where v=0 is the top, the painted sun's brightest central cluster is approximately **u=0.5014, v=0.2897**, measured at **37.85° above the geometric horizon**. Position was measured in the saved HDR, not inferred from the generation prompt.
- The rowhouse and tree skyline straddles roughly **v=0.52–0.57** (about **3.6°–12.6° below** the geometric horizon, depending on rooftop/tree and azimuth); the lower hemisphere fades to a warm dark nadir. It is a distant atmospheric backdrop, not a level-ground view or architectural geometry.
- In the included Blender environment preview the sun lies approximately toward **local +X**, with +Z up. **This is a source/preview convention, not a verified Unreal world azimuth.** Equirectangular and cubemap import conventions, material UVs and engine light transforms may rotate or flip it. In Unreal, rotate the sky material or sphere/cubemap until the *painted* sun aligns with the direction **toward the light source**, opposite the directional light's ray-travel direction. The integration brief says the existing light travels roughly south-southwest at ~38° elevation; check its actual transform rather than assuming a numeric yaw or sign. Rotate sky sphere and SkyLight cubemap coherently. Check north-facing windows, all four sides, and the seam after import.
- For the sphere, use an inward-visible/unlit sky material sampling the PNG or the HDR-derived cubemap with matching longitude orientation. Avoid physical collision, shadow casting, and unintended double exposure with an existing sky. For a SkyLight, try importing the `.hdr` as a long-lat cubemap and select it as a **specified cubemap**; verify the resulting Unreal asset type, orientation, lower-hemisphere policy, capture refresh and brightness in this project's engine version. A regular 2D PNG is not automatically a specified-cubemap asset. The pre-existing flat window cards may need to be removed or adjusted once the sky is placed; the distant rowhouse silhouette here does not reproduce the foreground buildings on those cards.
- The bright painted disc and Unreal's existing directional-light disc/atmosphere can double up. Align them or suppress one visible disc while retaining the physical light; evaluate this together with exposure and window lighting.

## Validation and limits

Saved PNG/HDR reloaded in Blender 5.2.3 as 2048 × 1024, with **zero maximum absolute difference between first and last columns**. Zenith and nadir row spreads were effectively zero (PNG max channel standard deviation < 0.000004). The selected sun cluster measured at 37.85–37.87° elevation. After Blender read-back, mean absolute RGB difference between HDR and PNG was ~0.0014 (RGBE quantization); these are not a substitute for Unreal image/color-management testing. Perspective renders of the sun, seam, both sides and zenith were inspected; the seam does not exhibit the earlier obvious mirrored tree, though the blend does soften/ghost a small region. Color banding, mip seams, sphere UV mapping, SkyLight cubemap import, shading energy, world rotation and final window framing remain unverified in Unreal.

## Generation prompts (built-in imagegen)

All three original candidates requested a **single, seamless 2:1 360° equirectangular** painterly late-afternoon environment, guided by the approved interior and existing window view, with a subtle distant brick-rowhouse/tree horizon, warm blue/peach/cream sky, and one centered sun glow. Candidate A emphasized airy high clouds and a dark lower hemisphere; B requested a quieter blue zenith, warm clouds and a subdued rowhouse horizon; C requested blue-to-apricot tones and fewer foreground landmarks. The retained B refinement asked for exact horizontal wrap and a sun at `u=.5, v=.289` with uniform poles. As generated, none achieved exact seam or required elevation; the documented Blender technical bake corrects those properties rather than pretending they were generated precisely.
