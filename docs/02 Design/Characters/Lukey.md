# Lukey: The Leaks

**Room:** Kitchen (headquarters), plus every leak in the house · **Glow:** `#4FA8FF`
**Scripts:** `Content/ICanFixHim/Data/Dialogue/lukey.json` · `podcast.json` · `epilogue.json`

> **Tone: *Ghosts* (UK).** Petty, vain, flirty, pun-saturated and very silly, with brief sincere moments that land and then go straight back to the bit. His death is a running joke, not a tragedy. Cutesy, never dark. Romance from minute one.

---

## The premise (Lukey's slice of the story)
- **The heroine** is a 29-year-old house flipper: ambitious, and deliberately a blank slate so the player brings the personality. She bought a house on Heartbreak Lane that was about to be **condemned**.
- **The deadline:** **30 days** to fix it for real, or the city tears it down.
- **The ghost:** every leak in the house is Lukey. He has no idea he was ever a person, and he won't find out until Act 4.
- **We only fix things for real.** There's no cosmetic flip.

## Who he thinks he is (Acts 1–3)
**Not a leak. THE leaks. Plural. "I'm a franchise."**
- He's spread across the house, and each leak is a "property":
  - **Kitchen sink:** headquarters, and where most of him is.
  - **Bathroom ceiling:** the penthouse. *"I look at the GROUT. Respectfully."*
  - **East wall:** the summer home, with a damp patch that looks like Elvis. Kevin the wasp nest lives there too.
  - **Hall radiator:** the holiday home, shared with a dead moth called Gerald.
- **Act 1: genuinely hostile.** He hates her on sight because she's a flipper here to profit off his home. Nicknames: "landlady," "Barbie." *"You're going to gut my house and sell it to a couple called Jasper and Pip."* *"Your hair's nice. Get out of my house."* It's petty, insulting and sabotaging, but funny, never cruel. He softens properly in Act 2.
- **Vain, territorial and (despite himself) flirty.** He assumes everyone fancies a bit of moisture.
- **He resents being fixed,** because every patched leak deletes a bit of him. *"You patched the bathroom. I can't feel my left side."*
- **His nemesis: Dennis the dehumidifier.** She buys Dennis to dry out the walls, and Dennis literally drinks bits of Lukey. *"You've bought an assassin. A little white plastic assassin. And you've NAMED it."* Dennis hums smugly, has a little green light, and appears in every act and most epilogues. In the storm his light turns red and Lukey finally respects him. When Lukey leaves as a friend, he leaves Dennis the kitchen.
- **Ghost rules, played for comedy:**
  - He can only affect water: taps, puddles, the kettle's steam, and Dan's shoes.
  - He can only go where the pipes go.
  - He writes messages in window condensation.

### Hints he explains away
The player should work it out long before he does:
- He hums Bon Jovi. *"It's in the water. I'm the water."*
- He does the Running Man without noticing.
- He has opinions on shoulder pads. "For pipes. Pipe shoulder pads."
- He says "since 1986," then "in my day."
- He dreams about leg warmers.

## Who he actually was (revealed in Act 4)
**Luke "Lukey" Moretti, aerobics instructor, died 1987, aged 34.**
- Mullet, teal Lycra, a headband, a big personality, *not* a plumber.
- To land a date with **Brenda**, he told her he was "great with his hands." Her sink was leaking. She went to get the wine, he dove under the sink to show off, slipped on the puddle and bonked his head on the U-bend.
- **He never got the date.**
- **His death mark:** permanently damp, his perfect blow-dry never dries, and he can't put down the wrench.
- **His reaction to the truth is mortification, not tragedy.** *"I died in LYCRA, sweetheart. In front of BRENDA."*

### Why he's stuck
**His unfinished business is the date he never got.** That's why the romance and his release are the same story: the heroine gives him the date.

## The outside world

### Inspector Dan, every Friday
A city inspector with a windbreaker, a moustache and terrible puns ("water-tight, like my alibi"). No relation to Lukey: he's **Lukey's pun rival and imagined love rival.**
- **Week 1:** "He said your foundation had GREAT BONES. That's building-code flirting."
- **Week 2:** Dan invites the heroine for coffee "about permits." Both his shoes end up full of water.
- **Week 3:** a pun-off via window condensation. *"DAN. YOUR PUNS ARE A PIPE DREAM."*
- **Ongoing:** Dan's shoes get soaked every Friday. In the epilogues he misses it when it stops.

### The podcast, every Wednesday: *Creaky Floorboards with Bev & Trish*
Two retired ladies who get everything wrong, until they don't. A ~30-second episode plays automatically on the radio at the start of each day, and nobody can be talked to until it ends.

| Ep | Title | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Catfish of Heartbreak Lane | Bev: a giant catfish lives in the walls. Trish: the homeowner is being *catfished*. They talk past each other. |
| 2 | Something in the Walls Is Singing | Neighbor **Mrs. Pruitt** (82, nine cats, one crossbow) hears Bon Jovi in the walls, "very flat, but very confident." Bev: "Classic catfish." |
| 3 | The Inspector | Bev & Trish interview Dan. "The only thing haunting that house is its water pressure." His shoes got flooded "on a dry day." Storm warning for next weekend. |
| 4 | We Went to the Library | The reveal, right before Lukey's Act 4: *"Local aerobics instructor Luke Moretti, 34, died while attempting to repair a sink on a first date."* "...So no catfish?" |
| Finale | Case Closed (Mostly) | Live from the porch after the inspection (only if it passed). "Never trust a man who says he's great with his hands." A bucket of water soaks Bev. |

### The storm
The forecast builds through Act 3 ("BIG STORM SATURDAY"), and Lukey is scared of floods "for leak reasons." In Act 4 the storm hits during the final repair.

## The arc: one leak per act
Each act is one playable day (one week). If the player skips Lukey for a week, his next conversation is the act they missed, so a player who skips can reach Act 4 too late and the house **fails** the final inspection (see "unfinished" below).

| Act | Title | Lukey is... | Key beats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | "I'm THE leaks." | Hostile, territorial, sabotaging, flirting against his will. Nicknames: **"landlady," "Barbie"** | "Oh, brilliant. Another one." He accuses her of flipping for profit, and she admits she is. "You're not just flipping a house. You're flipping ME." His rules: you fix something, I break something. Dan rivalry. **Dennis** arrives. Your handshake passes through him "like a sprinkler." |
| **2** | "Strictly professional." → **the fight** | Falling for you and pretending he isn't. Nickname: **"boss"** | A heart-shaped damp patch. "I'm wet in a SEXY way." Dennis has been drinking his summer home. Bon Jovi and 1986 hints get explained away. The east wall together ("concentrated, like an orange juice"). Dan's shoes flood. **Then the realtor call** (see below). |
| **3** | "It's not a date. (It's a date.)" | Down to one leak (you fixed the radiator during the silent treatment). Nickname: **"sweetheart"** | **The make-up:** "a house isn't just walls and grout, it's whoever's in it." Blackout candlelit dinner. Bon Jovi into a spoon. **First real hand-hold.** Storm forecast panic. Window pun-off with Dan. *"I like who I am when you're here."* |
| **4** | "Oh no. I'm a guy." | Mortified, then brave, then finally dry. Nickname: **her real name** (romance route) | The podcast reveal and Brenda. The storm repair ("Lefty-loosey is how I DIED!"). His hair dries for the first time since 1987 ("The VOLUME"). **The date he never got:** candles, 1987 shoulder pads, dancing to Bon Jovi. Then stay or go. |

Between acts you patch one leak off-screen: the bathroom ceiling after Act 1, and the radiator during Act 2's silent treatment. The east wall happens on-screen in Act 2, and the kitchen sink is the Act 4 finale.

### The mid-game conflict: a real difference in values
It isn't a misunderstanding. **She really is there to make money off his home, and he really does hate that.**
- **Act 1 sets it up.** She can admit it outright ("Enough to cover my mortgage and then some. That's the job."). He calls her a land shark with a business card.
- **End of Act 2: the realtor call.** Through the pipes, he hears her plan: *rip out the kitchen, open plan, grey floors, sell to whoever pays most, onto the next one.* Every tap in the house turns on.
  > *"This is a HOME, boss. People laughed in this kitchen. People had dates in this kitchen. Something happened in this kitchen. I don't know what. But it MATTERED."*
- **She has a real case too.** Everything she owns is riding on this flip. The player picks the line: explain the stakes, defend "houses are investments" (big friction), or admit she's been treating his home like a spreadsheet.
- **Three days of silence.** No drips, no puns, and even Dennis is uneasy. She fixes the radiator alone.
- **Act 3: the make-up is a compromise, not a surrender.** He admits houses cost money and she's good at fixing them; she can commit to fixing it **properly**, so whoever buys it gets a real home (sets `flag.lukey_realfix`). Or she can say she missed him, or that she still has to sell. Either way they end up at the blackout dinner.
- **Act 4 pays it off:** *"No wonder I hated you on sight. You were going to rip out this kitchen. The one place I nearly had a date."* It feeds straight into the final sell/stay/demolish choice.

## Hooks we're building in
1. **Solving the external problem together.** Each leak is a two-person job. He knows every drip from the inside; she has solid hands and can talk to Dan. Act 1 he sabotages, Act 2 he helps, Act 4 you're a team in the storm.
2. **A drip-fed mystery.** The player suspects he was human long before he does: the hints, the podcast, Mrs. Pruitt.
3. **Being seen.** He remembers what you chose: say you'll find something that works for "both of us" and he brings it up next week; be rude and he opens with a grudge. Nicknames move from "landlady" / "Barbie" → "boss" → "sweetheart" → your name.
4. **Physical progression (signature mechanic).** Fewer leaks means he's more concentrated and more solid. Act 1 your hand goes through him; Act 3 is the first hand-hold; Act 4 is the kiss.
5. **The leaks are his mood ring.** He drips faster when flustered, sprays when cross, turns every tap on when furious, and the heart-shaped damp patch grows when he's smitten (and shrinks to nothing in the fight).
6. **Obstacles and conflict:** he's a leak, there's a deadline, fixing him deletes him, and they genuinely disagree about what a house is for (the Act 2 fight). Plus jealousy over Dan and Dennis.
7. **Date set-pieces:** the east-wall repair ("the most romantic plumbing you've ever done"), the blackout dinner, the storm, the 1987 date.
8. **Visible consequences:** "*Lukey will remember that.*"
9. **Running gags that pay off:** Dan's shoes, Dennis the dehumidifier, Gerald the moth, Jasper and Pip, Bev's catfish, Mrs. Pruitt's crossbow.
10. **Epilogues** for every ending.
11. **A 30-day rhythm:** weekly repair → Wednesday podcast → Friday inspection.

> **Cut for now:** a collectibles / scrapbook system (too much dev work).

## Relationship tuning
- **Stats run 0–20 and are clamped** (the dialogue meters fill at 20).
- **Romance is generous.** Most kind or teasing choices give romance, since nearly everything with Lukey reads as flirting. Pure friendship options keep it platonic ("even if it's just as friends").
- **Thresholds are set above what random clicking gets you,** so endings feel earned. Simulated random play has a median of romance 15, friendship 12 and friction 8. A consistent romantic player maxes romance at 20, a consistent friend maxes friendship at 20, and a rude player maxes friction at 20.
- **The final choice is the player's**, from whatever they've earned:

| Option at the finale | Shown when | Ending |
|---|---|---|
| "Stay. With me. I'm in love with you." | romance ≥ 16 and friction < 8 | He stays |
| "I love you. That's why you should go." | romance ≥ 16 | He goes, loved |
| "Go. You got your date. You've earned it." | friendship ≥ 14 and friction < 8 | He goes, friends |
| "That's your call, Lukey." | always (one version shown) | friction < 8 → he goes as a friend; friction ≥ 8 → grumpy |

- He uses her **real name** in Act 4 if romance ≥ 12 going in. The Act 2 grudge opener plays if friction ≥ 4.

## Endings

### Lukey's ending
| Ending | Condition | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **He stays** (`romance_success`) | romance ≥ 16, friction < 8, "Stay with me" | Kiss, fade. *"Turns out my unfinished business was you. ...I'm not leaving, though. Have you SEEN this hair?"* |
| **He goes, loved** (`romance_failure`) | romance ≥ 16, "I love you, that's why you should go" | One last spin, and he leaves a heart-shaped drop on the floor. *"You deserve a fella with a pulse. And a dry floor."* |
| **He goes, friends** (`friend_success`) | friendship ≥ 14 and friction < 8, or "That's your call" with friction < 8 | "Best *frate* of my afterlife." Gone in a burst of sparkle and hairspray. |
| **He goes, grumpy** (`friend_failure`) | "That's your call" with friction ≥ 8 | *"Fixed it myself. Mostly. You held the torch."*, written in the window fog. |

**Unfinished:** if his Act 4 never happens before the end of week 4, Dan finds the kitchen leak at the final inspection and the house fails. The epilogue becomes the demolition: a frowning damp patch on demolition day, and a park fountain that drips and occasionally hums Bon Jovi. *"You should have finished the job."*

**After a romantic ending** (he stays, or he goes loved), the game offers **"See Lukey"**: a big portrait card with his ending title, then back to the title.

### The house choice (`epilogue.json`)
After the Day 30 hearing (Dan: "as dry as my sense of humor"; Mrs. Pruitt fires a celebratory crossbow bolt into the ceiling):

| Choice | He stayed | He passed on |
|---|---|---|
| **Sell** | Brenda (in her 70s) turns up at the open house and Lukey hides in the radiator for six hours. In your new apartment, the tap starts dripping: "A man's gotta make an entrance." | Brenda at the open house: *"I hope he's somewhere nice."* The kitchen never leaks again, which is a little sad. |
| **Stay** | The flipper finally has a home (Dennis lives in the cupboard). Dan's shoes get soaked every Friday. "One of these days I'll work out how." / "No he won't." | A good quiet house, except for faint Bon Jovi from the walls. Dennis keeps the kitchen. |
| **Demolish** | You take the sink. Lukey naps in it like a cat. The park fountain never stops dripping: "That's not me." | The park fountain drips forever. Nobody tries very hard to fix it. |

## Voice
**All-American, big, warm and grinning**, in the spirit of Emmett Cullen: a booming laugh, a chesty baritone, fast and bouncy when showing off, and soft, almost whispered when he's sincere. Full brief, Voice Design prompt and ElevenLabs fallback (**Liam**) in [[Voice Casting]].

Every one of his lines has a placeholder voice in the game now (free Kokoro TTS). It plays automatically when the line appears. Lines that say your name are recorded without it.

> **Accent vs script:** his lines were written in the *Ghosts* (UK) voice, and a few Britishisms will sound odd in an American mouth: "hoover", "advert", "pavement", "fancy", "soppy", "fella", "reckon", "That's flirting, that is". Worth Americanising before the final ElevenLabs record (then re-run the VO generator).
