# Lukey: The Leaks

**Room:** Kitchen (headquarters), plus every leak in the house · **Glow:** `#4FA8FF`
**Scripts:** `Content/ICanFixHim/Data/Dialogue/lukey.json` · `podcast.json` · `epilogue.json`

> **Tone: *Ghosts* (UK), but American.** An all-American guy in an American house: petty, vain, flirty, pun-saturated and very silly, with brief sincere moments that land and then go straight back to the bit. His death is a running joke, not a tragedy. Cutesy, never dark. Romance from minute one. Write him in American English (sidewalk, vacuum, flashlight, "have a crush on", "guy", "buddy").

---

## The premise (Lukey's slice of the story)
- **The heroine** is a 29-year-old house flipper: ambitious, and deliberately a blank slate so the player brings the personality. The player names her (`{player}`). She bought 12 Heartbreak Lane, which was about to be **condemned**.
- **The deadline:** **30 days** to fix it for real, or the city tears it down.
- **The ghost:** every leak in the house is Lukey. He has no idea he was ever a person, and he won't find out until Act 4. Nobody says plainly that he (or anyone else in the house) is a ghost before his reveal.
- **We only fix things for real.** There's no cosmetic flip.

## Who he thinks he is (Acts 1–3)
**Not a leak. THE leaks. Plural. "I'm a franchise."**
- He's spread across the house, and each leak is a "property":
  - **Kitchen sink:** headquarters, and where most of him is.
  - **Bathroom ceiling:** the penthouse. He drips on Tyler. Tyler hates it; Lukey loves it.
  - **East wall:** the summer home, with a heart-shaped damp patch. Kevin the wasp nest lives there too. It backs onto Malcolm's closet and is Malcolm's water supply.
  - **Hall radiator:** the vacation home, shared with a dead moth called Gerald.
- **Act 1: genuinely hostile.** He hates her on sight because she's a flipper here to profit off his home. Nicknames: "landlady," "Barbie." *"You're going to gut my house and sell it to a couple called Jasper and Pip."* *"Your hair's nice. Get out of my house."* It's petty, insulting and sabotaging, but funny, never cruel. He softens properly in Act 2.
- **Vain, territorial and (despite himself) flirty.** He assumes everyone has a crush on a little moisture. **His vanity is his hair:** chin-length, voluminous auburn ringlet curls that are always wet and never, ever dry (until the Act 4 fix, when they dry for the first time since 1987 and are ENORMOUS). His hair jokes are about his own hair, never anyone else's.
- **He resents being fixed,** because every patched leak deletes a piece of him. *"You patched the bathroom. I can't feel my left side."*
- **His nemesis: the dehumidifier.** She buys one to dry out the walls, and it literally drinks bits of him. *"You've bought an assassin. A little white plastic assassin with a carry handle."* **It is just an appliance:** no name, no personality, not sentient. It hums, its little light goes green or red (FULL), its bucket fills, it clicks off. All the comedy is Lukey resenting a *thing* (*"I dripped on the dehumidifier. It didn't even notice. It's a DEHUMIDIFIER."*). In the storm its bucket fills in seconds and he looks at it with something like respect; after the fix it clicks off with nothing left to drink. In his endings he banishes it to the garage, or tells you to put it in a lake.
- **Ghost rules, played for comedy:**
  - He can only affect water: taps, drips, the kettle's steam, the coffee pot, and your shoes.
  - He can only go where the pipes go.
  - He writes messages in window condensation.
  - He can't touch Wyatt (water and electricity), though this only comes up in Act 4 ("STAY OUT OF THE KITCHEN!").

### Hints he explains away
The player should work it out long before he does:
- He hums Bon Jovi. *"It's in the water. I'm the water."*
- He does the Running Man without noticing.
- He has opinions on shoulder pads. "For pipes. Pipe shoulder pads."
- He says "since 1986," then "in my day."
- He dreams about leg warmers.
- He bristles at "I'm good with my hands" in Act 1 ("EVERYBODY says that in this kitchen, right before something goes horribly—"), and in Act 3 he can't explain why a leak cares about table settings ("some dinner I never got to?").
- Malcolm has apparently been telling him, in riddles, that he's dead (*"Thou art a dead man's tears"*). Lukey's own script only pays this off in Act 4 ("Malcolm KNEW").

## Who he actually was (revealed in Act 4)
**Luke "Lukey" Moretti, aerobics instructor, died 1987, aged 34.**
- **In 1987:** teal Lycra, a big personality, *not* a plumber. That's what he **died in**. It is not what he looks like now (see "His look" below).
- **This was never his house. It was BRENDA'S.** He showed up early to pick her up for their first date (with a mixtape). Her sink was dripping. He told her, "Relax, babe. I'm great with my hands." She went upstairs to finish her hair; he dove under the sink, gave the pipe one big manly lefty-loosey, and about ninety pounds of cast-iron pipe came loose and fell on his head. Bonk.
- It's the pipe, and only the pipe: one big bonk. (The tile is Tyler's death, not his.)
- The newspaper: *"Local aerobics instructor Luke Moretti, 34, died in 1987 when a cast-iron pipe fell on his head as he attempted to repair a woman's sink before their first date."*
- **He never got the date.** And he'd been bragging that he'd "lived here thirty-nine years": he was in the house for twenty minutes.
- The pipe that killed him is still under the sink. In the storm he and the heroine fix it together. *"We've been roommates this whole time."*
- **His death mark:** the house rebuilt him out of its own plumbing. Copper-pipe limbs, a valve wheel where his heart should be, and hair that never dries. In Act 4 he looks down at himself and realizes: *"Thirty-nine years dressed as a SINK. And somehow that's the LESS embarrassing outfit."*
- **His reaction to the truth is mortification, not tragedy.** *"I died in LYCRA, sweetheart. Under BRENDA'S SINK."* Then: Malcolm KNEW. *"I thought he was just being GOTH about it!"* He briefly wonders whether everyone else in the house is the same, and refuses to think about it: *"One existential crisis per storm."* (He never says it about anyone else.)

## His look (matches the 3D model, `SK_LeakyPipes`)
Every appearance line in his script must match this. **No** headband, sweatband, wrench, Lycra or blow-dry in the present day.
- A big, broad, grinning man, early-to-mid 30s, **teal-green eyes**.
- **Chin-length, voluminous auburn/copper ringlet curls, permanently WET.** A short, full auburn beard.
- Neck, shoulders, arms and legs are **copper pipe** with green patina and brass couplings; brass rivets on his fingers.
- A soaked, see-through white ribbed **tank top with a red valve wheel** on the left chest.
- Faded blue **denim cut-offs** with drip stains, brown lace-up **work boots**.
- **Silver duct-tape patches** on forehead, cheek, chin, arms and shins; bandages on a forearm and a knee.

Where the script uses it: first sight in `v1` line 0; copper arm in `v1_end`; curls full of drywall paste in `v2_coffee`; brass rivets in `v3_hand`; copper-pipe legs and boots in `v3_end`; the "dressed as a sink" realization in `v4_reveal`; dry, ENORMOUS curls in `v4_fixed`; the date outfit in `v4_date` (fresh duct tape, polished couplings, double-knotted boots, an *ironed* see-through tank top); the duct-tape salute in `v4_fs`. The fallback mannequin colors/props in `look` were updated to match (white top, denim, brown boots, auburn hair; headband and wrench props removed).

### Why he's stuck
**His unfinished business is the date he never got.** That's why the romance and his release are the same story: the heroine gives him the date.

## The housemates
Lukey shares 12 Heartbreak Lane with three other guys, but **his story is about him and the heroine**. Rules (designer feedback, 2026-11-08):
- **Week 1 (Act 1, plus the `today` chatter): no mentions of Malcolm, Tyler or Wyatt at all.** The player may not have met them yet. (The only nod is the unnamed "last flipper... lasted one day" in `v1`, which is backstory, not a housemate.)
- **From week 2: at most two references to another housemate per act** (lines, exchanges, jealousy choices and asides all count), never the main topic.

| Act | References (max 2) |
|---|---|
| 1 | None. |
| 2 | 1. "WYATT'S! DON'T!" at the wiring while you open the east wall (narration, `v2_wall`). 2. Optional jealousy choice at the coffee (`v2_rivals`, names nobody). |
| 3 | 1. **The blackout is Wyatt's fault** ("That wasn't me!" It was him), narration only, `v3` line 0. 2. Optional jealousy choice at dinner (`v3_others`, names nobody). |
| 4 | 1. "Malcolm KNEW" (`v4_brenda`, with the optional `[Malcolm]` aside "I thought he was just being GOTH about it!"). 2. "WYATT! STAY OUT OF THE KITCHEN! ... Love you, buddy!" in the storm (`v4_storm`). |

Act 4 references are written to survive any play order (past tense or shouted from the kitchen), since another guy may already have been fixed by then.

### Jealousy (never punitive)
They aren't exclusive. Optional choices appear when she's been romancing the others (their `romance` stat). None appear in Act 1.
- **Act 2** (`>= 5` for any of them, at the coffee): "I live IN the walls. I hear EVERYTHING... It's a free market. We're rival franchises... I'm the only one who can fill your coffee pot from across the room."
- **Act 3** (`>= 7` for any of them, at the candlelit dinner): grown-up. "Nobody in this house is exclusive... But I'm your favorite. Don't answer that."

Both add romance and friendship; neither adds friction.

### Asides (hypertext)
Clickable words in his lines let the heroine make a one-off remark; he answers and the conversation returns to the exact line she clicked (`"aside": true` topics, `"return": true` nodes). They carry no stats, except the old `catfish` topic, which keeps its +1 romance.

| Act | Word (node) | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `[Gerald]` (`v1_aside_gerald`, only after `[franchise]`) | "He's been dead since the first Bush administration. He's thriving." |
| 2 | `[catfish]` (`v2_catfish`) | "I'm wet in a SEXY way." |
| 3 | `[Gerald]` (`v3_aside_gerald`) | "He's in a better place. The trash can." |
| 3 | `[table settings]` (`v3_aside_glasses`) | Why does a leak care about water glasses? "Since... some dinner I never got to?" A hint. |
| 4 | `[Malcolm]` (`v4_aside_malcolm`) | "I thought he was just being GOTH about it!" |
| 4 | `[cast iron]` (`v4_aside_pipe`) | The pipe that killed him. "We've been roommates this whole time." |
| 4 | `[ironed]` (`v4_aside_blazer`) | "I thought about it really hard. Turns out that works now. ...Still see-through, though. That part's a choice." |

## The outside world

### Inspector Dan: day 30 only
The city inspector does **not** visit every week (inspectors don't). He comes **once, on day 30**, for the final inspection (`inspection.json`). Lukey's weekly acts never put Dan on site. Instead:
- **Act 1:** the heroine tells Lukey the city inspects the house on day thirty and bulldozes it if it fails. He dreads it ("my headquarters, my penthouse, my summer home and my vacation home in one big wet pile").
- The "you patch a wall, I flood your shoes" rivalry is with **her**, not Dan.

### The podcast, every morning: *Creaky Floorboards with Bev & Trish*
Owned by `podcast.json` (see that file for the current episodes). Lukey's mystery is the main thread: Bev's catfish theory (he references it in Acts 2 and 3), Mrs. Pruitt hearing Bon Jovi in the walls (Act 3), "BIG STORM SATURDAY", and **episode 4's library reveal**, which quotes `v4_reveal` line 0 word for word (unchanged) and adds that it was Brenda's house.

### The storm
The forecast builds through Act 3 ("BIG STORM SATURDAY"), and Lukey is scared of floods "for leak reasons." In Act 4 the storm hits the whole house at once during the final repair.

## The arc: one leak per act
Each act is one playable day (one week). If the player skips Lukey for a week, his next conversation is the act they missed, so a player who skips can reach Act 4 too late and the house **fails** the final inspection (see "unfinished" below).

| Act | Title | Lukey is... | Key beats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | "I'm THE leaks." | Hostile, territorial, sabotaging, flirting against his will. Nicknames: **"landlady," "Barbie"** | He slides out from under the sink on a wave (first look per the model). "Oh, great. Another one." "Last flipper wanted everything white. Lasted one day." He accuses her of flipping for profit, and she admits she is. "You're not just flipping a house. You're flipping ME." His rules: you fix something, I break something. **The deadline:** she tells him about the day-30 inspection; "I'm good with my hands" makes him flinch. **The dehumidifier** arrives. **The touch:** she reaches past him for the tap, slips in his puddle, and he lunges to catch her: one second of cold wet arm, then just water. "Can't even catch you. Can't even touch me." No housemates. |
| **2** | "Strictly professional." → **the fight** | Falling for you and pretending he isn't. Nickname: **"boss"** | A heart-shaped damp patch. He misses the penthouse view. The dehumidifier has him in the hydrangeas ("I'm MULCH"). "I'm wet in a SEXY way." Bon Jovi and 1986 hints get explained away. The east wall together ("concentrated, like an orange juice"; "WYATT'S! DON'T!"). **Coffee on the kitchen floor:** he fills the pot from across the room; "Is this a coffee date?" ("In my day we called this grabbing a milkshake... In my day?"); "Where does a flipper go when she runs out of house?" **Then the realtor call** (see below): "WHITE CABINETS? Why don't you paint the whole place Swiss Coffee!" |
| **3** | "It's not a date. (It's a date.)" | Down to one leak (you fixed the radiator during the silent treatment). Nickname: **"sweetheart"** | **Wyatt's blackout** (narration only: "That wasn't me!"; it was him; sets `flag.lukey_blackout`). "I wasn't sulking. I was RESTING." **The make-up:** "a house isn't just walls and grout, it's whoever's in it. Right now that's me, a wasp called Kevin and, unfortunately, you." Candlelit dinner, water glasses filled to the same line, Bon Jovi into a spoon. **First real hand-hold** (brass rivets, cold). Storm forecast panic. **The window:** the pasta steam fogs the glass and "L +" appears, then gets rubbed out ("That was condensation. It's science."); finish it and it reads "L + THE KITCHEN SINK + U (MAYBE)". *"I like who I am when you're here."* |
| **4** | "Oh no. I'm a guy." | Mortified, then brave, then finally dry. Nickname: **her real name** (romance route) | The podcast reveal: Brenda's house, the cast-iron pipe (sets `flag.lukey_human`). He realizes he died in Lycra and has spent 39 years "dressed as a SINK." "Malcolm KNEW." The storm repair, under the same pipe ("Lefty-loosey is how I DIED!"). His curls dry for the first time since 1987 and are ENORMOUS ("The VOLUME"). **The date he never got:** candles, an ironed see-through tank top, a mixtape labeled FOR ~~BRENDA~~ you, dancing to Bon Jovi. Then stay or go. |

Between acts you patch one leak off-screen: the bathroom ceiling after Act 1, and the radiator during Act 2's silent treatment. The east wall happens on-screen in Act 2, and the kitchen sink is the Act 4 finale.

### The mid-game conflict: a real difference in values
It isn't a misunderstanding. **She really is there to make money off his home, and he really does hate that.**
- **Act 1 sets it up.** She can admit it outright ("Enough to cover my mortgage and then some. That's the job."). He calls her a land shark with a business card.
- **End of Act 2: the realtor call.** Through the pipes, he hears her plan: *rip out the kitchen, open plan, white cabinets, gray floors, sell to whoever pays most, onto the next one.* Every tap in the house turns on.
  > *"This is a HOME, boss. People laughed in this kitchen. People had dates in this kitchen. Something happened in this kitchen. I don't know what. But it MATTERED."*
- **She has a real case too.** Everything she owns is riding on this flip. The player picks the line: explain the stakes, defend "houses are investments" (big friction), or admit she's been treating his home like a spreadsheet.
- **Three days of silence.** No drips, no puns, and the dehumidifier's bucket stays bone dry. She fixes the radiator alone.
- **Act 3: the make-up is a compromise, not a surrender.** He admits houses cost money and she's good at fixing them; she can commit to fixing it **properly**, so whoever buys it gets a real home (sets `flag.lukey_realfix`). Or she can say she missed him, or that she still has to sell. Either way they end up at the blackout dinner.
- **Act 4 pays it off:** *"No wonder I hated you on sight. You were going to rip out this kitchen. The one place I nearly had a date."* It feeds straight into the final sell/stay/demolish choice.

## Hooks we're building in
1. **Solving the external problem together.** Each leak is a two-person job. He knows every drip from the inside; she has solid hands and a deadline (the day-30 inspection). Act 1 he sabotages, Act 2 he helps, Act 4 you're a team in the storm.
2. **A drip-fed mystery.** The player suspects he was human long before he does: the hints, the podcast, Mrs. Pruitt, "I'm good with my hands", the table settings.
3. **Being seen.** He remembers what you chose: say you'll find something that works for "both of us" and he brings it up next week; be rude and he opens with a grudge. Nicknames move from "landlady" / "Barbie" → "boss" → "sweetheart" → your name.
4. **Physical progression (signature mechanic).** Fewer leaks means he's more concentrated and more solid. Act 1 he tries to catch you and you go straight through him into the puddle; Act 3 is the first hand-hold; Act 4 is the kiss.
5. **The leaks are his mood ring.** He drips faster when flustered, sprays when cross, turns every tap on when furious, and the heart-shaped damp patch grows when he's smitten (and shrinks to nothing in the fight).
6. **Obstacles and conflict:** he's a leak, there's a deadline, fixing him deletes him, and they genuinely disagree about what a house is for (the Act 2 fight). Plus a little optional jealousy over the housemates (Acts 2–3 only).
7. **Date set-pieces:** the east-wall repair ("the most romantic plumbing you've ever done"), coffee on the kitchen floor, the blackout dinner and the window, the storm, the 1987 date.
8. **Visible consequences:** "*Lukey will remember that.*"
9. **Running gags that pay off:** your flooded shoes, the dehumidifier (an appliance he hates), Gerald the moth, Kevin the wasp, Jasper and Pip, "Swiss Coffee", Bev's catfish, writing in the window fog, the coffee pot he fills from across the room.
10. **Epilogues** for every ending.
11. **A 30-day rhythm:** one playable day per week: morning podcast → one act per guy. The only inspection is the final one on day 30.

## Relationship tuning
- **Stats run 0–20 and are clamped** (the dialogue meters fill at 20).
- **Romance is generous.** Most kind or teasing choices give romance, since nearly everything with Lukey reads as flirting. Pure friendship options keep it platonic ("even if it's just as friends").
- **Thresholds are set above what random clicking gets you,** so endings feel earned. Simulated random play has a median of romance 15, friendship 12 and friction 8. A consistent romantic player maxes romance at 20, a consistent friend maxes friendship at 20, and a rude player maxes friction at 20. The optional jealousy choices and the `catfish` aside can add a point or two of romance/friendship on top. (Re-simulated after the 2026-11-08 revision: same reachable endings and the same medians.)
- **The final choice is the player's**, from whatever they've earned:

| Option at the finale | Shown when | Ending |
|---|---|---|
| "Stay. With me. I'm in love with you." | romance ≥ 16 and friction < 8 | He stays |
| "I love you. That's why you should go." | romance ≥ 16 | He goes, loved |
| "Go. You got your date. You've earned it." | friendship ≥ 14 and friction < 8 | He goes, friends |
| "That's your call, Lukey." | always (one version shown) | friction < 8 → he goes as a friend; friction ≥ 8 → grumpy |

- He uses her **real name** in Act 4 if romance ≥ 12 going in. The Act 2 grudge opener plays if friction ≥ 4.

## Endings

### Lukey's ending
| Ending | Condition | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **He stays** (`romance_success`) | romance ≥ 16, friction < 8, "Stay with me" | Kiss, fade. *"Turns out my unfinished business was you. ...I'm not leaving, though. Have you SEEN this hair?"* |
| **He goes, loved** (`romance_failure`) | romance ≥ 16, "I love you, that's why you should go" | One last spin, and he leaves a heart-shaped drop on the floor. *"You deserve a guy with a pulse. And a dry floor."* |
| **He goes, friends** (`friend_success`) | friendship ≥ 14 and friction < 8, or "That's your call" with friction < 8 | A two-finger salute off the duct tape on his forehead. "Best *frate* of my afterlife." Gone in a burst of sparkle and warm steam that smells faintly of copper. |
| **He goes, grumpy** (`friend_failure`) | "That's your call" with friction ≥ 8 | *"Fixed it myself. Mostly. You held the flashlight."*, written in the window fog. |

**Unfinished:** if his Act 4 never happens before the end of week 4, Dan finds the kitchen leak at the final inspection and the house fails (it passes only when all four maladies are fixed). The epilogue becomes the demolition, with a last walk through the house; in the kitchen, a frowning damp patch on demolition day. *"You should have finished the job."* The lot becomes a park with a fountain that drips and occasionally hums Bon Jovi.

**After a romantic ending** (he stays, or he goes loved), the game offers **"See Lukey"**: a big portrait card with his ending title, then back to the title.

## Voice
**All-American, big, warm and grinning**, in the spirit of Emmett Cullen: a booming laugh, a chesty baritone, fast and bouncy when showing off, and soft, almost whispered when he's sincere. Full brief, Voice Design prompt and ElevenLabs fallback (**Liam**) in [[Voice Casting]].

Every one of his lines has a placeholder voice in the game (free Kokoro TTS). It plays automatically when the line appears. Lines that say your name are recorded without it.

> **Accent vs script:** the v2 script is written in American English, so the Britishisms flagged in the v1 placeholder pass ("hoover", "advert", "pavement", "fella", "reckon"...) are gone. The placeholder VO was regenerated for the v2 lines on 2026-11-08. **The 2026-11-08 designer-feedback revision changed or moved many voiced lines; re-run `Tools/Audio/generate_vo.py` and `Tools/Editor/import_vo.py`.**
