# Lukey: Leaky Plumbing

**Room:** Kitchen (under the sink) · **Glow:** `#4FA8FF` · **Dialogue:** `Content/ICanFixHim/Data/Dialogue/lukey.json` (prototype v1. Needs rewriting to match this doc.)

> Tone: **cutesy-sad, not dark.** Silly and punny first, warm and bittersweet later, always with a joke nearby. Think Pixar, not horror. No glitchy, creepy or horror-style presentation.

---

## The premise (Lukey's slice of the story)
- **The heroine** is a 29-year-old house flipper: ambitious, and deliberately a blank slate so the player brings the personality. She bought a house on Heartbreak Lane that was about to be **condemned**.
- **The deadline:** she has **30 days** to fix it up or the city tears it down. That would be bad for her wallet and worse for the ghost living in the plumbing.
- **The ghost:** the leak under the kitchen sink is Lukey. He's been there so long he believes he *is* the pipe.
- **We only fix things for real.** No cosmetic flips. If we're fixing it, we're fixing it properly.

---

## Who he was
**Luke "Lukey" Moretti, 1953-1987, died aged 34.**
- Town swim coach and the neighbourhood's self-appointed handyman. He was *terrible* at it, but very confident.
- Mullet, short shorts, a Walkman permanently clipped to his hip, a Bon Jovi devotee.
- Single dad to **Danny**, who was about to turn 8.
- Flirted with everyone: the mail carrier, the lunch lady, a mannequin once (by accident).
- Big heart, bigger ego, and he **never finished a single project.**

## How he died (lightened)
It was **Danny's 8th birthday party, 1987.** The kitchen sink sprang a leak right before the cake came out. Lukey dove under the sink with a wrench ("Two seconds, buddy!"), slipped on the puddle and bonked his head on the U-bend.

- When he finally remembers it, his reaction is **mortified more than tragic:** *"Of all the ways to go. Under a SINK. In front of a CLOWN."*
- The quiet sad part: **he missed the candles.** Danny blew them out without him.

## Why he's stuck
**He never finished the job.** That's his whole personality, and it's what's holding him here:
- He never fixed the leak.
- He never taught Danny to swim.
- He never said goodbye.
- **A wrapped present is still taped up behind the cabinet:** a pair of swim goggles and a birthday card in his awful handwriting.

## Why he thinks he's a pipe
Thirty-nine years under a sink will do that to a guy.
> *"Lady, I've been a pipe longer than you've been alive. I know who I am."*

He believes fixing the leak will destroy him, so at first he **resents you** for trying.
> *"Fix me and I'm DEAD, lady. Well. Deader. More pipe-ier."*

## The outside world (what Lukey and the heroine talk about)

### Inspector Dan: every Friday
- Grown-up **Danny, now 47.** A by-the-book city building inspector who became one "so no house ever hurts anybody again."
- Corny. **He tells the exact same plumbing puns Lukey does.** Puns are hereditary.
- **This is the running gag and the emotional reveal:**
  - Week 1, Lukey: *"Who does this guy think he is, stealing my material?"*
  - Week 3, Lukey: *"...that's MY joke. Why does he have MY joke?"*
- Each weekly inspection is a checkpoint for the real-world problem: what still fails code and what's due by next Friday.
- Open question: does Dan ever learn the truth? Current lean: **ambiguous.** He feels a drip on his head at the end and grins.

### The podcast: *Creaky Floorboards with Bev & Trish*, every Wednesday
Two retired ladies investigate local "mysteries" and get **everything wrong with total confidence.** Lukey listens through the pipes. Each episode gives the pair something juicy to talk about, and it nudges a memory loose.

| Ep | Title | Bev & Trish say... | Lukey says... |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "The Heartbreak Lane Hauntings" | "...and some say a MERMAID lives in the plumbing." | *"I'm flattered."* |
| 2 | "Who Was the Man in the Short Shorts?" | First hint that a man died at the house in 1987. | *"Short shorts were IN, Trish."* |
| 3 | "The Birthday Party Mystery" | They interview a party guest. **The clown.** The clown remembers. | (silence, then) *"...I know that clown."* |
| 4 | "Solved?!" | Triumphantly announce the wrong answer: **the mob.** The house's ghost "dropped a mug" on their mic. | Very, very proud of the mug. |

The running gag pays off when they go from always wrong to almost right.

### The storm forecast
It builds all game and hits in week 4.
- Week 1: "70% chance of rain this month."
- Week 2: "Tropical system forming."
- Week 3: headline **"BIG STORM SATURDAY."** Lukey panics about water and doesn't know why.
- Week 4: the storm hits. Your repairs have to hold, and you hold the kitchen together.

## The arc: four weeks, four tones

| Week | Tone | Lukey is... | Key beats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1: "I'm a pipe."** | Silly, bratty | A cocky little jerk, proud to be a leak. Resents you. Pitched as **bratty and full of himself, not cruel.** | Sprays you on purpose and floods your shoes. Jealous of the new dishwasher ("Who's HE?"). Tries to haunt you but is too wholesome to be scary. First inspection; Dan: "Water damage. Like father, like... something," and he finds himself hilarious. Lukey sabotages the week's repair. |
| **2: "Do pipes have birthdays?"** | Curious, goofy, warm | Noticing things a pipe shouldn't. Smells cake. Hums Bon Jovi, then panics: *"Pipes don't have FAVORITE SONGS."* | Finds his own Walkman wedged behind the cabinet and knows every word to the tape. Starts flirting for real: tries to stop leaking for a whole afternoon to impress you. *"Wait. Do I have... legs?"* Grudgingly helps with the repair. **The fight** (see below). |
| **3: "I was a guy. I had a kid."** | Bittersweet, still funny | Remembering: coach, dad, Danny. Works out that **Dan is his son** at Friday's inspection, when Dan tells *the* joke. | The storm forecast scares him. He remembers the party, the cake, the slip: *"...under a SINK."* The quiet sad beat: he missed the candles. Asks for help: *"I gotta finish something. I just don't know what."* Now he's **teaching you** the repairs. |
| **4: "Let's finish the job."** | Hopeful, cozy, a big emotional finish | Determined. Teaching you to fix the pipe properly: *"Righty-tighty, sweetheart. Lefty-loosey is how I died."* | You **find the present** behind the cabinet. **The storm hits** and you hold the kitchen together; the pipe holds. **Final inspection:** you give Dan the goggles and card. Dan reads it, laughs and cries. Lukey watches, **finally finished.** |

### The fight (late week 2 / early week 3)
Lukey finds out that fixing the leak might mean he **passes on.** He accuses the heroine of only wanting the house fixed so she can sell it, and only being nice to him to get the job done. The player chooses how to respond. The **make-up scene** afterwards should be one of the most loved scenes in the route.

## Hooks we're building in

### 1. You need each other to solve the problem
The romance grows out of **teamwork on the condemned house.** Each partner can do something the other can't:
- **He knows the house from the inside:** *"There's a crack behind the dishwasher. Trust me, I've been IN it."* He spots problems, knows where the pipes run, and remembers what Gary the bad contractor did in 1986.
- **She can do what he can't:** go outside, talk to Dan, buy parts, hold a wrench with a solid hand.
- **Each week's repair is a two-person job,** and how they work together tracks the relationship:
  - week 1 he sabotages it
  - week 2 he grudgingly helps
  - week 3 he teaches her
  - week 4 they're a real team holding back the storm

### 2. A mystery that drip-feeds
Every conversation ends on a small hook that pulls the player into the next one:
> *"Hey... what's a 'Danny'? I just said it. I don't know why I said it."*

The podcast, Dan's jokes and objects around the house are all clues. The player is solving him alongside him.

### 3. Being seen
- **Callbacks:** he remembers what you told him days ago. *"You said you hated cilantro. I remembered. I'm a pipe, I have nothing else going on."*
- **The mirror:** the heroine is a blank slate, so Lukey describes her back to the player based on their choices. *"You're the kind of person who fixes stuff for real, huh?"*
- **Nicknames that change as you get closer:** **"lady" → "boss" → "sweetheart" → her actual name.** The first time he says her real name is a big moment.

### 4. Firsts, and a physical progression (signature mechanic)
**He gets more solid the more human he becomes:**
- Week 1: your hand passes right through him.
- Week 2: touching him feels like cold water.
- Week 3: **the first time he can hold your hand** is a huge moment.
- Week 4: the kiss lands because the player has been waiting for it.

Visually he goes from pipe-man (current look) → wearing his 1987 clothes (mullet, short shorts, Walkman) → a softly glowing ghost of himself.

### 5. The leak is his mood ring
The leak shows how he's feeling without him saying it:
- **Drips faster** when nervous or flirty. *"It's humid in here. Shut up."*
- **Sprays** when he's angry.
- **Goes dry and quiet** when he's sad.

Players learn to read it, and it's free comedy. This can be driven from the existing malady drip FX.

### 6. Obstacles and a fight
Built-in obstacles: **he's dead, there's a deadline, and fixing him might end him.** Plus the one real argument (above) and its make-up scene.

### 7. Date set-pieces
Special scenes that break the routine:
- **Blackout candlelight dinner:** he "cooks" by directing you, badly.
- **VHS movie night:** an 80s tape he's obsessed with and narrates over.
- **"Pool day" in the bathtub:** he finally gets to coach someone again.
- **Storm night:** holding the kitchen together.

### 8. Choices with visible consequences
- Some scenes are missable, and some lines only appear because of something you said earlier. That's replay value.
- Show it on screen: **"Lukey will remember that."**

### 9. Running gags that pay off
- **The dishwasher rivalry** pays off when he "forgives" it in his goodbye.
- **Dan's hereditary puns** turn from gag to reveal.
- **Bev & Trish** go from always wrong to almost right.

### 10. Epilogue
A short scene for every ending, plus a final postcard or scrapbook page showing life afterwards.

### 11. A daily rhythm with limited time
A **30-day calendar** where each day you choose how to spend your time:
- repair something
- talk to Lukey
- search the house
- listen to the podcast (Wednesdays)
- prep for inspection (Fridays)

Limited days make time with him feel precious and give the game a "one more day" loop.

> **Cut for now:** a collectibles / memory scrapbook *system* (too much dev work). Story objects like the Walkman, the Polaroid and the goggles still appear as **scripted beats** in dialogue, just not as a collection menu.

## Endings

### Lukey's ending
| Relationship | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **In love** | **He stays** as a ghost boyfriend. Still wet, still flirty, still terrible at handyman work. *"I finished the job. Doesn't mean I gotta clock out."* |
| **Friends** | **He passes on.** A warm goodbye, a goofy salute, one last splash. Dan feels a drip on his head at the door and grins. |
| **Low / rude** | He **still passes on.** Helping him finish the job is always enough, but he's a bit grumpy about it: *"Fixed it myself. Mostly. You held the flashlight."* Kept **soft, not bitter.** (Open question: do we want a harsher version?) |

### The house choice (final decision)
No cosmetic-flip option. We fix it for real or not at all.

| Choice | If Lukey passed on | If Lukey stayed (love) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fix for real and sell** | Clean, happy flip. Dan signs off on the house. | He's sad to leave and comes with you. You move, and he haunts the sink in your new place. Comedy epilogue. |
| **Stay** | You keep the house, a quiet home with a lot of history. | **The romance ending.** You live there with him and Dan comes to Sunday dinner. The flipper finally has a home. |
| **Demolish** | Bittersweet. You honour what happened there, and the lot becomes a little park with a drinking fountain that never stops dripping. | He moves with you (see "sell"), a little homesick, and the park fountain drips in his honour. |

## Open questions
1. Is the death light enough? (A bonk under the sink at a birthday party, played as mortifying.)
2. Does Dan ever find out the truth, or do we keep it ambiguous?
3. Is there a harsher rude-player ending, or is every route kind?
4. Exactly how bratty is week-1 Lukey?
5. What's the heroine's default name, and does the player rename her?
