# Tyler: Pink Tile

**Room:** Bathroom (the 1957 pink tile wall) · **Glow:** `#FF7AB8`
**Script:** `Content/ICanFixHim/Data/Dialogue/tyler.json`

> **Tone: *Ghosts* (UK), American house.** Snooty, commanding, vain and jealous, with brief sincere moments that land and then go straight back to the bit. His death is a running joke, not a tragedy. Cutesy, never dark. Romance from minute one.

> **Voice:** old-money American with crisp military-school diction. Composed, clipped, snooty. "Ma'am." "Outstanding." "Carry on." "Noted. Logged." "That's an order." He's no longer British (the v1 prototype was). He never swears and never raises his voice except in ALL CAPS on one word. When he's flustered he gets *more* formal, not less.

---

## Who he is
**Tyler Whitmore, 27.** Heir to the Whitmore real-estate dynasty ("American royalty"): Whitmore Tower, Whitmore Plaza, Whitmore Grand, Whitmore Parking Structure B, all with the name in gold.
- Raised by **eleven nannies** (consecutively; they kept saying no to him, then they kept leaving), an etiquette coach (**Madame Fontaine**) and **Fort Bellamy Military Academy** from 13 (youngest cadet captain in its history; his father donated the gymnasium the same year, "unrelated").
- Crisp, proper, snooty. Gives orders. Says "ma'am." **Has always gotten his way and has never been told no and had it stick.**
- Father's first rule: *"Never fall in love with a property."*
- **Vanity: his pink hair, and his body.** The pompadour USED to be rose quartz, hand-mixed by a colorist in Manhattan. Since his "posting" it has drifted to the exact pink of the tile, which he finds unbearable: *"We are NOT the same pink. We are, unfortunately, the same pink."* He does two hundred push-ups a day at oh-five-hundred (Fort Bellamy standard) and calls the glazed tile on his chest a "tan" ("It's a GLAZE. Very Mediterranean.").
- Calls Wyatt his **batman** (officer's orderly). Salutes the dehumidifier (an ordinary appliance, no name, no personality: "the only disciplined thing in this house"). Salutes everyone. **Nobody ever salutes back.**

### Appearance (matches the model `SK_PinkTile`)
A **hugely muscular bodybuilder**, late 20s, square jaw, blue eyes, smug half-smile, clean-shaven. **Hot-pink short pompadour with faded sides**, the same saturated pink as the tile. **Shirtless:** from the neck down he's glossy hot-pink square tile with white grout lines, flaking into bare skin at the collarbones, sternum and wrists, and creeping up his temples as small tile scales. **Hot-pink drawstring gym shorts, white crew socks, pink canvas high-tops.** No blazer, crest, loafers, phone, watch or cashmere.

Why: he died straight from the gym, in leg-day shorts, socks and high-tops (the Act 3 video shows him in a tank top too). The tank top is now "the BATHROOM". He can only move pink things, so he can't put on a (white) towel, let alone a shirt. When the wall loses a tile, he loses a tile (bare skin shows); when she sets one back, his fills in.

### How he died (2024)
He bought 12 Heartbreak Lane to flip it: Swiss Coffee white on every wall, white subway tile, a little wooden sign that says BREATHE, sell by Sunday. On day one he filmed a tour for his followers ("Whitmore Nation," 312 of them) of how hideous the 1950s pink bathroom was, and slipped on the tile he despised. Now he **is** that cracked, antique pink tile, which is the cruellest possible irony for him.

### What he believes (Acts 1–2)
He knows he's Tyler Whitmore. He refuses to accept that he's dead. He talks around it:
- *"Since my... reassignment."* · *"I'm between engagements."* · *"Temporary posting. Classified."* · *"It's been a very long weekend."*
- He knows he's somehow attached to the tile and hates it. He "feels it sympathetically."

### Hints he explains away (the player should get there first)
- **"Watch your step, ma'am. The floor's slick."** He says it every time she leaves. The floor is bone dry. (It's the last thing he said on camera.)
- He walks very carefully, the way people walk on a wet floor.
- He rubs the back of his head ("old polo injury"). Flinches when she touches the cracked tile; clutches his ribs when she pries one off ("sympathy pain, military training").
- Nobody salutes back: not the mailman, not the woman next door with the crossbow, not the dehumidifier. "It's a very rude neighborhood."
- He has **no reflection**: in Act 2 he salutes the mirror and nobody is in it ("It's an old mirror. They lose their silver.").
- Day 1: she steadies herself on his forearm after his "watch your step"; for one second it's solid, glazed tile, then her fingers sink through like cold bathwater ("I had you. Briefly. That one was a me problem. Don't log it.").
- He can only move pink things ("a condition of my posting"), so his Operation Clean Slate troops are a pink soap, a pink loofah and a pink rubber duck, and he can't wear a shirt.
- His "tan" is glazed tile with grout lines across the abs.

### His reveal (Act 3): the video
She searches the address for the listing and finds **"WORST BATHROOM IN AMERICA?! (Flip Day 1)"**, posted 2024, **312 views** (one per tile: "even my audience is pink"). A sweaty, very alive Tyler, straight from the gym in a tank top and the same shorts, socks and high-tops he's wearing now, with a rose-quartz pompadour: *"Behold: the ugliest bathroom in America... White. Subway. Basic. SELL. Watch your step, this tile is slick as—"* CLONK. The last frame is the ceiling. One comment: *"is he ok"*, two likes. Sets `flag.tyler_video`.

*"I'm not between engagements, am I. ... I've been wearing my LEG-DAY SHORTS for two years. ... Nobody salutes back because nobody can SEE me. I'm the tile, ma'am. I'm the Pepto-Bismol."*

### The conflict
At first he's on her side, but worse: *"Rip it out. White. Subway. Basic. SELL."* He's her **temptation and her mirror**, because she flips for profit too ("You remind me of me. That ought to worry you more than it does."). Being the old tile makes him notice that it's mud-set on wire lath, handmade, has survived 70 years, and was **loved** by the families who lived here, in a way his cheap white flips never were ("I once lost a whole wall of subway in eleven months. The adhesive came from a gas station.").

The tile remembers the families: **Hal and Ruthie** (Hal set it in June 1957 and pencilled on the mortar *"SHE WANTED PINK. BUILT TO OUTLAST US BOTH."*), a girl with chicken pox in 1964 who counted all 312 tiles, a ring hidden in the soap dish in 1979, a teenager who dyed her hair pink in the sink with Kool-Aid in 1994, and a woman in 1987 who kept a bottle of wine for a date that never happened (Brenda; a quiet easter egg for players who know Lukey's story, never named or pointed at).

**His unfinished business is being told no, and loved anyway.** He thinks it's finishing the flip ("Rip me out, sell it, and I'm dismissed. Honorably."). The real fix is restoring the tile, which means telling him **"No, Tyler. It stays."** He's furious, then moved.

### Jealousy (the most jealous in the house, because he's vain)
He keeps a **ledger** of how long she spends elsewhere, and he sulks theatrically. It's comedic friction: it never locks content.
- **Week 1:** no mention of the other guys at all. He's jealous only in the abstract (the repeat-visit line: *"I'm not jealous of wherever you're going next. I'm keeping a LEDGER."*).
- **Week 2:** if she's just come from Lukey or Malcolm with romance ≥ 6: *"Forty-one minutes. I counted the drips. Or the sighs."*
- **Week 3:** if she's just come from Lukey or Malcolm and either has romance ≥ 10: *"You smell like a sink. Or a sonnet. I counted the tiles. Twice."*
- **Week 4:** if she went to Lukey first in the storm: *"The KITCHEN. During a CRISIS."*

### Cross-character budget
Designer rule: **Act 1 mentions no other housemate. Acts 2–4 have at most two housemate references each**, never the main topic, spent mostly on jealousy.

## Arc (one act per week)
**Ghost rules / progression:** Act 1 her hand finds his arm solid for one second, then sinks through (cold bathwater). Act 2 his fingers pass through her shoulders. Act 3 his hand is solid ("cool and smooth, like glazed tile warmed by the sun"). Act 4 he can hold a bucket of mortar, and the tiles she sets back reappear on him.

1. **Act 1 (week 1): the ally.** No housemates mentioned. He steps out of the wall (shirtless, tiled, enormous). "Ma'am. Thank God. A professional." The plan: rip it all out. Choices: ally / tease his hair (it used to be rose quartz, now it matches the tile exactly) / call the tile charming (the first "no": `flag.tyler_no`). His numbers and Father's rule; the nanny story (`seen.v1_nanny`, called back in Act 4). Second PT: push-ups on the bath mat (choices: what are you / need a spotter? / put a shirt on). He salutes the dehumidifier. "Watch your step. The floor's slick." She steadies herself on his forearm; solid for a second, then cold bathwater. The floor is bone dry.
2. **Act 2 (week 2): Operation Clean Slate.** Openers: rival jealousy / "you called it charming" callback / grudge (friction ≥ 3). The battle plan in pink bath toys; his "tan" is glaze. She pries a test tile ("Ow." A tile-sized patch of skin shows on his ribs). Behind it: *SET BY HAL FOR RUTHIE* (`flag.tyler_hal`). He starts noticing the craftsmanship. She mentions the day-30 inspection; he drills the bath toys for it and salutes the mirror, which is empty (no reflection). Doing the numbers on the tub: "I came in to film the before. I never got to the after."
3. **Act 3 (week 3): the video.** Openers: rival jealousy / candlelit power cut (`flag.lukey_blackout`, nobody blamed). The tile remembers the families. She finds the video (he died in his leg-day shorts); the reveal (`flag.tyler_video`). He decides his business is finishing the flip and makes his first ever request. First hand-hold. Storm warning; storm protocol ("I'll be the chain"); optional Wyatt exchange (with a `flag.wyatt_spoke_up` variant: he ordered Wyatt to keep the bathroom fan running through the storm and Wyatt said NO; "He was right. I'll tell him. In a memo.").
4. **Act 4 (week 4): the storm.** Uses her real name if romance ≥ 12. Rain behind the wall; tiles popping off ("I can feel every single one"), leaving bare skin on him. Jealousy if she went to Lukey first (with a `flag.lukey_human` variant). He orders her to let it go: "Let the tile go, ma'am. Let ME go." She tells him **no** (all paths get there; the "rip it out" choice is a temptation she backs out of). He's furious ("I have never been told no in my LIFE."), then moved. The two of them save the wall all night; every tile she sets back reappears on him; he drill-sergeants the grout ("Hold the LINE!"). The cracked tile gets a **gold seam** (like the towers, except nobody paid for it), or he sets the last line himself, or it gets slapped with grout. At dawn he inspects the wall like a parade ("Let the inspector come on day thirty."). She salutes him: "Somebody saluted back." The final choice.

## Relationship tuning
Stats run 0–20, clamped. Same thresholds as [[Lukey]]. Simulated random play (after the cross-reference trim): median romance 15, friendship 15–16, friction 7. A consistently romantic player ends at romance 20, friction ≤ 1; a consistently friendly player at friendship 20, friction 0; a consistently rude player at friction 20.

| Option at the finale | Shown when | Ending |
|---|---|---|
| "Stay. That's an order. I'm keeping you too." | romance ≥ 16 and friction < 8 | He stays |
| "I love you. That's why I'm letting you go." | romance ≥ 16 | He goes, loved |
| "You're a good man, Tyler Whitmore. Dismissed." | friendship ≥ 14 and friction < 8 | He goes, friends |
| "That's your call, Tyler." | always (one version shown) | friction < 8: friends · friction ≥ 8: grumpy |

## Endings
| Ending | Title | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| `romance_success` | **Tile Death Do Us Part** | "That's another no. To me leaving." Father said never fall in love with a property; he fell for the property AND the owner. Fade: *The bathroom was pink. So was everything else.* "I still think it looks like Pepto-Bismol. ...I love it. Tell no one." |
| `romance_failure` | **Tile We Meet Again** | Told no and loved anyway, he's free. A kiss, a salute, she salutes back: "Somebody saluted back." Gone, leaving a gleam on the gold seam, like rose quartz. |
| `friend_success` | **Grout Expectations** | "Dismissed. Honorably." Salute returned. "Somebody saluted BACK. Log it. Log it TWICE." He marches into the morning light and doesn't look down once. The vanity light stays on for a week. |
| `friend_failure` | **Grout of Here** | "You kept it for the inspection. I know the difference. I'm a Whitmore." He leaves furious and moved; one bottom-row tile clicks crooked out of spite. It's the best tile in the house. |

Every ending restores the tile: the house is only fixed for real if it's restored, not replaced.

## Voice
**Old-money American heir with military-school diction** (see the voice note at the top): a crisp, commanding, parade-ground baritone, snooty and composed, every sentence ending like an order. He gets *more* formal when flustered, and splutters when complimented. He's no longer British. ElevenLabs fallback: **Roger** (alt: **Eric**). Full brief and Voice Design prompt: [[Voice Casting]]. Not voiced in the game yet.
